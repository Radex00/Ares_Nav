## 1. How to cite sources in the write-up (guide)

**a) In the text: a number in square brackets** at the end of the sentence that contains the fact.

> The Viking orbiters sent back 52,663 images of Mars **[2]**.

- Number each source in the order it first appears.
- If a sentence uses two sources: **[2, 3]**.
- The team's own opinions or conclusions do not get a number.

**b) At the end: a "References" list** with the same numbers.

**c) Format for each type of source** (in English, because the submission is in English):

| Type | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| NASA web page | Organization. (Year or n.d.). *Page title*. URL (accessed 14 Nov 2026) | NASA Science. (n.d.). *Viking 1*. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viking-1/ (accessed 14 Nov 2026) |
| News article | Organization. (Year, Month Day). *Title*. URL | NASA/JPL. (2022, December 21). *NASA Retires InSight Mars Lander Mission After Years of Science*. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/... |
| Image | Exact credit. (Year). *Title* (PIA number or ID) [Image]. Mission, instrument. URL | NASA/JPL-Caltech/UArizona. (2024). *HiRISE Studies the Dust on InSight* (PIA26515) [Image]. Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, HiRISE. https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/hirise-studies-the-dust-on-insight/ |
| Scientific paper | Surname, Initial., ... et al. (Year). Title. *Journal*, volume, article number. DOI | Golombek, M., Warner, N. H., Grant, J. A., et al. (2020). Geology of the InSight landing site on Mars. *Nature Communications*, 11, 1014. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14679-1 |

**d) Important rules**

1. **An image's credit is copied exactly**, for example "NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona", and goes next to the image or in its caption.
2. **The NASA logo is never used**, nor anything that suggests NASA sponsors the project.
3. **AI-made images carry the "AI-generated" watermark** and are listed in section 6 of this file.
4. **Explain things in your own words.** If you copy a sentence, put it in quotation marks with its number.
5. **If two sources disagree, say so.** There is a real example in section 2 (the Viking lander photos).

## 5. Data calculated by the team (and how)

These numbers are not written in any source: we calculated them from data in the sources.

| Data | How it was calculated |
|---|---|
| Zone A scale: 0.306 m/pixel, **339 × 299 m** | PIA26515 scale bar: 20 m = 65.3 pixels. 1106 × 977 pixels × 0.306 |
| New zone scale: 0.562 m/pixel, **635 × 719 m** | "100 m" scale bar = 178 pixels. 1131 × 1280 pixels × 0.562 |
| Position of each marker (in %) | The object's pixel in the image ÷ the image size × 100. The turquoise objects (InSight, shields) were located by their color |
| Approximate coordinates of each zone | Starting from InSight (4.502° N, 135.623° E) [10] and moving with the scale. On Mars, 1° of latitude ≈ 59.16 km (radius 3,389.5 km [11]). We assume north is up, as in projected HiRISE images [8] |
| Crater in the new zone: about 50–60 m | Measured on the image with the scale |
| Hazards "Rockier terrain" and "Crater (slope)" | The terrain data come from [10] and [8]; treating them as a **hazard** is a choice of the simulation, and their info card says so |
| 360° horizon from the 290° panorama [13] | 6446 px ÷ 290° = 22.2 px per degree. We removed the black and white edges (no data), leaving 6246 px = **281°** of real photo. The missing **79°** were filled by blending the two edges as a mirror image, so the strip joins without a seam; that stretch is labeled "NO PHOTO IN THIS DIRECTION". The top and bottom edges fade into the sky and ground colors of the photo itself |
| Vital Room readings | **Simulated**: they move randomly around the real values from [11], [14], [15], [16] and [17]. The temperature rises and falls like a day at InSight, but one "sol" lasts 4 minutes. The green / orange / red thresholds and the 3 events (dust storm, solar particle event, marsquake) are choices of the simulation; the page says "(simulation)" |
| Horizon orientation | **Not calibrated:** the source does not say which way the camera was facing. The landscape is decorative; the floating points and the compass do use the real bearings from the map |

## 7. Tools

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), used through Claude Code:** help writing the code and finding and checking sources. Every code file starts with a comment that says so. It also drew the rover, the drone and Rick (section 6), corrected the spelling of the team's logs and translated them into English (the texts are the team's; see `HISTORIA.md`).
- **Python with the Pillow library:** image editing. Cropping the 2018 HiRISE map, making its thumbnail, and building the 360° horizon from the InSight panorama (see sections 3 and 5).
- **Google Chrome (headless mode):** automatic tests. It loads the page with no window and checks that everything works, including at three screen sizes.
- **Voicebox** (https://github.com/jamiepine/voicebox), a free app that runs on the computer, **with the Kokoro voice model** (https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M): the AI voice of the logs. Its Whisper transcription was used to check the pronunciation.
- **FFmpeg** (https://ffmpeg.org): joins each log with its P.S. and converts it to MP3 with the "AI-generated voice" metadata.
- **GitHub and GitHub Pages:** the code is stored on GitHub, and the page is published with GitHub Pages.
- **The page uses no external libraries:** HTML, CSS and JavaScript written by hand. The fonts are ones that already come with the computer (Consolas, Segoe UI).
